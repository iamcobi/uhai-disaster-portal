# Storm-layer sources, interpretation and remaining prediction gap

## New datasets actually used

**Native outage history:** 273,399 EAGLE-I source county/time observations: 267,091 observations in 2024 for nine Houston-area counties, plus 6,308 in the Harvey window. The 2024 file covers the year; the GUI selects July 8–August 1 for Beryl and May 16–June 7 for the derecho, with end dates excluded. The Harvey window is August 25–September 15, 2017. These windows include recovery and potentially unrelated incidents. Raw source timestamps are UTC. Customers are electric service accounts, not people or households.

Source: Christa Brelsford, Sarah Tennille, Aaron Myers, Supriya Chinthavali and colleagues, ORNL EAGLE-I recorded electricity outages dataset, https://doi.org/10.6084/m9.figshare.24237376 ; native files https://ndownloader.figshare.com/files/53581661 (2024) and https://ndownloader.figshare.com/files/42547828 (2017). License: CC BY 4.0, https://creativecommons.org/licenses/by/4.0/ . Data are subsetted and summarized here; metadata files record byte ranges and retrieval date October 23, 2026. The source files were sorted by county in 2024 and time in 2017, allowing range downloads instead of whole national files.

The map uses each county's highest recorded `customers_out` in the selected window. Repeated snapshots are not added as distinct customers or outages. Missing county/time rows remain unknown, never assumed to be verified zero outages. Observation frequency is shown as source-record availability, not utility/customer coverage. Older feed coverage can severely underrepresent real impacts: Harvey's low recorded Harris count must not be read as evidence that Harris had few true outages. No restoration duration or new-storm probability is inferred from gaps.

Where 2024 peak `customers_out` exceeds the supplied `total_customers` at that row, an explicit source consistency flag is retained. Those counties are gray in the outage map and excluded from the cooling-priority rank. This is an illustrative quality rule, not proof of a particular source error. The raw peak and denominator remain visible for inspection. Gray does not mean safe or no outages. Red uses log-scaled relative recorded counts and is not a probability.

**Houston 311 requests:** 46,715 selected mapped records from the published Harvey and 2024 archives, plus 1,603 additional Harvey cleanup, medical-evacuation and storm-damage requests. The 2024 parser scanned 481,130 source rows; two short malformed rows were excluded. Records are filtered to the selected date windows and the study coordinate rectangle. Relevant duplicate source IDs were removed, but separate reports about the same incident or caller can remain. All addresses, phone/contact fields, narrative free text and tax IDs were omitted from the delivered 311 extract. Map coordinates are rounded to four decimals.

Official archive index: https://www.houstontx.gov/311/servicerequestdata.html . Files: https://hfdapp.houstontx.gov/311/311-CRIS-Public-Data-Extract-D365-YTD-compressed-2024.txt and https://hfdapp.houstontx.gov/311/311-Public-Data-Extract-Harvey-clean.txt . Source download dates and parser counts are retained in the metadata JSONs.

Tree-removal heatmaps include `Tree Removal` and `Severe Storm Tree Removal`, not tree planting or routine trimming. Removal requests do not verify a tree fell, hit a person, damaged a line, or was caused by that storm. Storm-debris reports do not necessarily contain only tree debris. Electrical hazards are not verified outages. Building-safety/code complaints are not confirmed collapses; building break-in categories are excluded from that layer. Additional cleanup and evacuation categories were extracted only for Harvey. An empty corresponding 2024 layer means uncollected data, not verified zero need. Requests do not count distinct injured people, service users or completed cleanup jobs.

The source largely reflects City of Houston reporting coverage, not comprehensive reporting throughout Greater Houston. Call access, service availability, old backlog and reporting behavior affect counts. Reports retain actual geocoded map points. For placement weights only, records are grouped to their nearest represented ZIP-area point using an approximate latitude/longitude distance. Those counts are explicitly not exact ZIP membership. One ZIP representative can collect reports from neighboring ZIPs. Heat colors show relative report density within the current filter; they do not support numerical comparisons between independently scaled filters or storms.

**Damage descriptions:** 156 NOAA Storm Events source records in selected regional windows, plus 90 NWS Houston/Galveston local storm reports for Beryl, archived by Iowa Environmental Mesonet. The included NOAA bulk extract has no HGX July 2024 records; the supplement prevents silently treating that gap as no damage. Source narrative mention filters cover flood/water, trees, buildings/roofs/windows and power/poles. Mention tags are simple keyword searches, not a causal classifier or a damage footprint. The original text, weather event category and source link are retained. Some NOAA records cover a forecast zone rather than a county or point.

Sources: https://www.ncei.noaa.gov/access/storm-events-database/ ; https://www.weather.gov/hgx/beryl2024 ; the archived NWS local reports query https://mesonet.agron.iastate.edu/cgi-bin/request/gis/lsr.py?wfo=HGX&sts=2024-07-08T00%3A00%3A00Z&ets=2024-07-10T00%3A00%3A00Z&fmt=csv . Local storm reports can be preliminary and describe approximate report locations. The raw 90-report source subset and metadata are supplied.

**Utility report snapshot:** The official CenterPoint map's public read-only event URL was inspected: https://centerpoint.datacapable.com/datacapable/v2/p/centerpoint/map/events . Official map: https://tracker.centerpointenergy.com/map/ . This is an observed public app endpoint, not a documented stable API contract. The refresh button makes one request and retains the cached snapshot on failure. It is not background monitoring.

The bundled feed's newest event update is September 23, 2026 at 01:30:44 UTC, despite retrieval on October 23. It is therefore explicitly marked old. Each report also has an individual freshness check: event updates over two hours old or implausibly future dated get gray markers. That is a prototype freshness rule, not proof that an old report has been resolved. Merely fetching a feed now does not prove its event contents are current. No-data responses are not certified absence of outages. The vendor field `numPeople` is displayed only as provider-reported affected count, not as a population estimate. Unknown cause stays unknown. Planned reports remain labeled planned. Premise hashes and miscellaneous provider metadata were removed from the delivered snapshot.

**County boundaries:** Census 2020 TIGERweb county polygons, layer 82, https://tigerweb.geo.census.gov/arcgis/rest/services/Census2020/tigerWMS_Census2020/MapServer/82 . Simplified by coordinate precision for display. ZIP internal representative points are assigned a county by point-in-polygon; this is not exact allocation of a cross-county ZIP's residents or customers.

## Four independent resource searches

All roles consider the same 14 existing candidate centers; 12 pass the illustrative FEMA SFHA point screen. Actual permission, road access, building/wind safety, staffing, generator power and capacity remain unverified. Roles may share a location without a shared-capacity constraint. Solutions are best only among these candidates under the stated assumptions, not anywhere in Houston.

- Ambulance staging: maximize published HHS electricity-dependent Medicare weight reached within a modeled ten-minute base-to-area drive. This is a vulnerability proxy, not injury or ambulance dispatch demand. A site count is a staging budget, not a verified count of staffed ambulances.
- Repair dispatch: maximize selected-window tree-removal, storm-debris and electrical-hazard report weight within ten modeled base-to-area minutes. These are service reports, not utility-confirmed faults or line-work orders.
- Cooling hubs: prioritize HHS electricity-dependent counts multiplied by the percentile rank of county peak reported outage counts among unflagged county observations; use area-to-hub driving. This is an explicit ranking proxy, not an outage probability or people actually needing cooling. Invalid/missing county records receive no known priority weight, not an assertion of zero need.
- Community/medical posts: use the fixed Harvey reported-household-member context layer, with area-to-hub driving. It does not become a Beryl impact forecast when the storm selector changes.

Ambulance and community weights are fixed historical/context layers; the storm filter changes repair and outage-related weights. A separate closure control excludes one candidate. Travel slowdown is an assumed multiplier, not a flood-closure forecast. Driving to ZIP points omits dispatch, pickup, rescue, queues and service time. Only two fixed existing public ER examples are shown; there is no complete ER inventory, clinical assignment, hospital capacity model, hospital construction or relocation optimizer. The original people/supply GUI remains a separate section with its original assumptions and model limitations.

## Why future probabilities remain unavailable

No future neighborhood outage or tree-strike probability model has been fitted or validated here. The interface never relabels a past report density or a hand-built proxy as a probability. In particular, a county outage count cannot be redistributed into apparently precise neighborhood probabilities. Reports and warning co-occurrence cannot establish that infrastructure was improperly built or maintained. Asset inspection, age, standards, maintenance, vegetation exposure, weather and confirmed failure-mechanism records would be needed for such claims.

A defensible future model would need time-matched utility outage labels at the intended neighborhood/circuit resolution, verified non-outage observations, weather forecasts available before the prediction time, tree exposure/condition and relevant asset data. Training should hold out entire storms, evaluate meaningful baselines and probability calibration, and handle reporting coverage and infrastructure changes. Tree strike on a person or line requires its own event labels; a tree-density map is not that label. The public street-tree inventory we found is partial and old, not a complete tree-health/failure inventory.

The warning-matched Zenodo outage archive was investigated (https://zenodo.org/records/22651795). Its regional subset stopped in 2022 despite the stated 2015–2024 scope, so it was not used for the Beryl/derecho map. It contains only positive processed outage hours overlapping warnings, making absent rows unsuitable as verified negative training examples. Native EAGLE-I source records were used for the maps instead.
